# Archetype Map (v6.84) — what the item set actually supports

Bottom-up read of the full registry (stickers, pillars, bases, Same-Powers,
packs, the Old Joker's bargains) plus the engine rules they key on. Not a
list of intentions — a list of what a skilled player could deliberately
assemble TODAY, with honest viability calls.

The currencies that matter: **survival** (piles alive), **information**
(peeks/tells), **deck knowledge** (thin deck = countable deck), **coins**
(convert to items), **score** (piles × smallest — prestige only; it never
keeps you alive). Any "archetype" is a claim about converting one of these
into another at better-than-generic rates.

---

## 1. THE BURY ENGINE — well-supported (the game's richest family)

*Strategy:* shrink the remaining deck mid-deal; every buried card is a draw
you never have to survive, and it ends deals faster.

- **Payoffs (bury sources):** Quick Bury, Snowball, Club Snob, Rank Roots,
  Clean Bury, Streak Bury, Dense Bury, Club Thin, Empty Ranks, Void
  Tribute, Pauper's Club, Club Dig, Sticker Harvest, Burrow, Last Resort.
- **Enablers:** ♣ shaping (Change to ♣, Transmute→♣), rank duplication for
  Rank Roots, purging ranks for Empty Ranks, Streak discipline.
- **Viability:** HIGH — deepest bench in the game, multiple price points,
  ungated seeds (Quick Bury). Assemblable by mid-run in most seeds.
- **Counterweight:** real and elegant — Second Wind and Phoenix UNDO burials
  (returned to deck), Deep Pockets pays you NOT to bury, and burying feeds
  the smallest-pile score DOWN (buried cards sit under piles, but killing
  the deck early caps how big piles grow). Also curse Trapdoor reverses it.
- **Reachability:** no composition threshold needed; ♣-density variants one
  Transmute away.

## 2. THE INFORMATION ENGINE — well-supported, and the convergent default

*Strategy:* see the next card as often as possible; a seen card is a free
correct guess.

- **Payoffs:** Scout, Twin Spark, Pillar/Base Scout, Spade Snob, Tell,
  Spade Whispers, Queen's Eye, Static, Last Rites, Eight Ball, Pauper's
  Spade, Spade Peeker, Lone Eye, Club Oracle, Same Tell, Empty Purse,
  Kamikaze, Demolish, Same Peeker, Second Sight.
- **Enablers:** ♠ shaping (most peek stickers are ♠-locked), royal keeps
  (Queen's Eye), 8-density (Eight Ball).
- **Viability:** HIGH — too high, honestly. Peeks are the strongest currency
  and a third of the item set pays in them.
- **Counterweight:** WEAK. Only the self-costed peeks charge anything
  (Kamikaze a pile, Demolish a pillar, Empty Purse the purse, Lone Eye the
  power slot). The sticker peeks are free. This is why other archetypes
  collapse into this one: peeks never ask you to change how you play — they
  just make the default play better.
- **Reachability:** trivial.

## 3. THE SAME/RANK-FLOOD ENGINE — well-supported since v6.76, genuinely distinct

*Strategy:* duplicate ranks on purpose, call Same constantly, and make each
Same detonate.

- **Payoffs:** Rank Flood (the anchor: every top becomes the called rank →
  the NEXT Sames are trivial — self-accelerating), Same-Safe, Column
  Tie-Safe, the tolerance family (Close Call / Royal Pair / Perfect Ten /
  Same Suit Safe), every Same-Power via more frequent triggers, Recharge
  Shield/Cell, Tap Power, Power Surge, Twin Spark, Rank Roots, Diamond Echo.
- **Enablers:** Chorus, Rank Setter, Transmute (13 same-rank? no — same
  SUIT; rank duplication comes from ±Rank stickers), Old Joker's EIGHTS
  (aces+2s→8s: the single biggest rank-duplicator in the game), Rank Purge
  (removing off-ranks), Crazy Eights as both payoff and signpost.
- **Viability:** HIGH mid-late — the v6.76/v6.80 batch plus gating actually
  delivered this: the tolerance family is buyable, Rank Flood gates behind
  samesCalled 60 (you unlock it BY playing the archetype), and Eights/Chorus
  exist as activators. This is the game's one true alternate playstyle.
- **Counterweight:** real — duplicated ranks make DIRECTIONAL calls worse
  (more ties you didn't call), the tolerance pillars eat your column slots,
  and flooding tops to one rank makes every non-Same call on the board a
  coin flip. You genuinely play differently.
- **Reachability:** Crazy Eights' "8s most common" is reachable essentially
  only via the Eights bargain (random encounter) or heavy ±Rank investment —
  it's the archetype's lottery jackpot, not its plan.

## 4. THE THIN-DECK/PURGE SCHOOL — well-supported, quietly the strongest

*Strategy:* permanently shrink the deck; a 30-card deck is countable,
predictable, and every remaining card is chosen.

- **Payoffs:** intrinsic (counting accuracy — the histogram IS the payoff),
  plus Empty Ranks, Royal Sanctuary (no 2s), Void Tribute (no {suit}),
  Scarce Suit steering, Majority Rule (purge toward 50%).
- **Enablers:** the Purge slot + its whole economy (Bulk Rate, Flat Purge,
  Purge Coupon, On the House), Rank Purge, Sacrifice, Long Odds, Old
  Joker's Cut/Purge/Eights, Transmute (suit consolidation).
- **Viability:** HIGH — the archetype the shop's whole discount economy
  exists to serve. The v6.76 composition pillars finally gave it explicit
  payoffs beyond counting.
- **Counterweight:** real — purges cost coins on a climbing ladder (the
  ladder IS the counterweight), Deep Pockets pays for a FAT deck, and a
  thin deck reshuffles sooner (paid redeals). Old Joker's Purge attaches
  curses.
- **Reachability:** no-2s (Royal Sanctuary) = 4 purges: cheap, realistic.
  Zero-of-a-suit (Void Tribute) = up to 13 removals or Transmute-assisted:
  late-run but real. 50% mono-suit (Majority Rule) = one Transmute plus a
  few converts: realistic mid-run. These thresholds are honest.

## 5. THE MONO-SUIT PROJECT — half-built, leaning on two items

*Strategy:* consolidate the deck into one suit (or empty one out) and buy
the suit-keyed safeties.

- **Payoffs:** Majority Rule, Void Tribute, Same Suit Safe (denser
  suit-matches), the Guards (denser triggers), stage-suit alignment.
- **Enablers:** Transmute (the workhorse — 13 cards in one stroke), Change
  to X stickers, Wild Suit, purges, Suit Setter.
- **Viability:** MEDIUM — everything routes through Transmute (rare, gated
  behind bossesBeaten 8) or a long stream of 1-coin converts. When Transmute
  shows, the archetype is ON; otherwise it's a trickle.
- **Counterweight:** stage suits rotate (Mamma's phases punish mono-suit),
  suit-restricted stickers lose homes (a mono-♥ deck can't carry ♣ tools),
  and Scarce Suit's shield runs AWAY from your majority.
- **Reachability:** 50% easily; 100% aspirational; ZERO of a suit realistic
  late with Transmute.

## 6. THE COIN/HEART ECONOMY — half-built as an archetype, fine as a lane

*Strategy:* hearts pay; coins buy the run.

- **Payoffs:** Bonus Coin, Payout, Last Coin, Collector, Compound, Loose
  Change, Deep Pockets, Heart Snob, Heart Choir, Heart Bonus, All Hearts,
  Envy, Highest Heart, Excavator, Pauper's Heart, Heart Tax, Heart
  Demolish, Dividend, Greedy, Gambler, Insurance, Last Licks, Guardian.
- **Enablers:** Change to ♥, Transmute→♥, the store itself (coins only
  matter if shelves are worth buying — Rare Hunter/Freebie amplify).
- **Viability:** MEDIUM — the pieces are everywhere (largest single family)
  but the LOOP is circular: coins→items→more coins is only a build if the
  items bought serve some OTHER archetype. Money is fuel, not a strategy.
- **Counterweight:** none inside the family (its members are mostly the
  autoplay drips from the Principle-1 audit); the Pauper family is the one
  interesting inversion (be BROKE on purpose) and it fights this lane head-on.
- **Honest read:** this is a support lane wearing an archetype's clothes.
  Half its members failed Principle 1 for exactly that reason.

## 7. THE SCORE/DIAMOND STACK — half-built, and the payoff is prestige

*Strategy:* maximize piles × smallest — big equal piles, ♦ weights, anchors.

- **Payoffs:** Heavy, Massive, Anchor, Diamond Anchor, Massive Diamond,
  Diamond Echo, Diamond Boost, Diamond Lifeline, Same Heavy, Fourth Seat,
  Streak Size, Ballast, Donate, Diamond Distribution, Pauper's Diamond.
- **Enablers:** Change to ♦, Transmute→♦, Guardian-style keeps.
- **Viability:** MEDIUM mechanically — the pieces assemble fine. But score
  buys NOTHING in-run: it's leaderboard prestige. So this archetype is real
  only for players chasing bests; for everyone else these items are noise.
- **Counterweight:** the real one — building size means NOT building
  survival, and endless score-chasing eventually kills the run. That's a
  legitimate risk-appetite archetype… for the audience that wants it.

## 8. THE PAUPER — coherent micro-archetype, thin bench

*Strategy:* stay under 10 coins on purpose; spend everything, always.

- **Payoffs:** the four Paupers, Empty Purse.
- **Enablers:** none needed (being broke is free); the store's cheap tier.
- **Viability:** MEDIUM — five items is enough to matter, and "buy
  everything cheap immediately" is a genuinely different shop posture.
- **Counterweight:** built-in and honest — no savings means no rare buys, no
  purge ladder, no reroll money. The cleanest tradeoff family in the game.

## 9. THE CURSE-EATER — aspirational-only

*Strategy:* accept curses for value, then harvest or cleanse them.

- **Payoffs:** Curse Harvest (the ONLY positive curse payoff).
- **Enablers:** Devil's Deal, Old Joker's Purge/Duplicate bargains, Cleanse
  as the off-ramp, Bouncer as the opt-out.
- **Viability:** LOW — one payoff item cannot carry an archetype. You can't
  SEEK curses (they arrive by bargain or bane), and one pillar's bury+peek
  doesn't outweigh what the medium/severe curses cost. Claimed by the item
  text, not supported by the math.
- **Missing piece:** a second and third curse payoff (e.g. anything that
  scales with cursed cards HELD), and a reliable curse source you choose.

## 10. THE STREAK COLUMN — aspirational-only

*Strategy:* funnel every guess through one column.

- **Payoffs:** Streak Size, Streak Bury. **Enablers:** none — nothing makes
  in-column guessing safer to sustain (Tell/Tie-Safe help incidentally).
- **Viability:** LOW — two items, and the reset-on-leaving cost fights the
  board's basic need to spread deaths. A real mechanic, not yet a build.

## 11. THE JOKER PORTFOLIO — aspirational-only

*Strategy:* collect ★ Jokers as universal safety.

- **Payoffs:** the Joker itself (always-safe calls), Wild Aces adjacency.
- **Enablers:** store card slot (1/53 at 25 coins), mystery joker outcome,
  A Star for Your Flags (pillars→Joker!), joker caps per tier.
- **Viability:** LOW-MEDIUM — jokers are individually excellent but capped,
  expensive, and Legendary bans them entirely. The Flags trade is the one
  deliberate route and it costs your whole pillar row. More a windfall than
  a build.

## Cross-cutting honesty

**Well-supported:** Bury (1), Information (2), Same/Rank-Flood (3),
Thin-Deck (4). **Half-built:** Mono-suit (5), Coin lane (6), Score stack
(7), Pauper (8). **Aspirational-only:** Curse-eater (9), Streak (10),
Joker (11).

**Orphaned goodstuff** (belongs to no archetype, floats into every run):
Second Wind, Phoenix, Revive, Fourth Seat, Upheaval, Ballast, Diamond
Distribution, Shuffler, Bouncer, Ditto, Reactor, Escape Hatch, Freebie /
Rare Hunter / Bulk Rate (the shop trio serves every build equally, which is
to say none), Wild Sticker, Sticker Spray, Link Shuffler, Rekindle. None of
these are bad — they're exactly the "generic goodstuff" a drafting game
fills with. But nearly all the DEFENSIVE structure (saves, revives,
geometry) is archetype-less, which means every archetype defends the same
way — and defense is most of the game.

**Distinctness:** archetypes 1, 2 and 4 substantially collapse into one
"see more, survive more, draw less" core — their currencies (peek, bury,
thin) are three prices for the same good. Archetype 3 (Same/Rank-Flood) is
the one that genuinely plays differently at the moment of calling. 5 feeds
3 and 4 more than it stands alone. 7 is orthogonal but optional-by-design;
8 is a shop posture, not a deal posture.

**Bottom line:** the game today rewards building toward a strategy in
exactly ONE direction — the Same/rank-duplication engine — and that one is
real: a committed player calls differently, shapes differently, and unlocks
its anchor by doing so. Everything else converges on peek/bury/thin
survival goodstuff at different vendors: efficient, fun, but one approach
wearing four coats. The v6.76 composition pillars gave thin-deck and
mono-suit genuine END STATES to build toward (no-2s, zero-suit, 50%-suit),
which is the right scaffolding — what's missing is (a) counterweights on
information (peeks are free power in every build), (b) a second archetype
that changes CALLS the way the Same engine does, and (c) payoffs that make
score matter to a run rather than to a leaderboard. The curse-eater and
streak lanes are one to two items short of existing at all.
